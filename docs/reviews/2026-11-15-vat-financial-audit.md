# Ghana VAT financial-control audit — 15 November 2026

## Scope and conclusion

The original VAT work was merged as PR #93, commit `c1ce84acedeac97fb67d8f52d1c3902f90686ac0`, and deployed to the team inspection environment before this audit. This review independently traced registration, collection, statutory input eligibility, refunds, revenues, return preparation, filed-period controls and recovery. Tests use isolated synthetic businesses; no real shop transactions, credits, registrations or GRA returns were changed by this audit.

**The first pass had material errors. Passing its original tests did not establish compliance.** The corrections below improve the working-paper and transaction controls; they are not GRA certification or an opinion that a particular taxpayer's return is correct. Actual fiscal API approval, current return-field mapping, taxpayer eligibility and primary-law confirmation of Act 1177 remain release gates. A future reviewer should reproduce the counterexamples, not rely on a “zero errors” assertion.

## Missed errors and corrections

| Finding | Risk and original error | Correction / verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F01 — High | Late-entered historic turnover used the date entered as the requirement date, potentially postponing VAT by a month or more. A future registration certificate date could also suppress an earlier statutory trigger. | Determine the first evidenced historical crossing; retain requirement, detection and effective dates separately. A backdated discovery cannot postpone liability. Flag untaxed sales after the legal date as filing blockers. Test an October crossing entered in November, original rolling-window boundaries, and a later active registration followed by discovery of an earlier crossing. This last case was also missed in the first audit fix and corrected during final review. |
| F02 — High | A general forecast was persisted before the statutory month-end test and remained effective even if withdrawn before month end. | Reassess the general forecast from the configuration effective at month end. Actual turnover crossings remain sticky. Counterexample: October forecast withdrawn before 31 October must not activate November VAT. |
| F03 — High | The six-month input-credit check used the first day of the claim month, allowing late claims. | Exact calendar-month deadline from evidenced accrual date (section 49(4)). Later accrual requires evidence; pre-registration deductions first accrue when eligible at registration. Test the exact deadline and the day after it. |
| F04 — High | Supplier details and an eligibility checkbox did not implement blocked expense categories, pre-registration conditions or residual mixed-supply rules. | Section 50 exclusions for vehicles/parts, entertainment and memberships; documented business exceptions; exempt/private denial. Section 51: first registration period, qualifying stock/capital on hand, four-/six-month acquisition windows. Section 52: residual mixed ratio below 5% gives no credit, above 95% gives full otherwise-allowable credit; financial institutions require direct attribution. Keep original invoiced amounts and applied ratio. |
| F05 — High | External supplies entered as turnover affected registration but were omitted from output VAT. | Dedicated external-sale evidence joins output and classification schedules. Registered periods reject threshold-only entries. Reconcile pre-existing turnover entries by ID without double counting. Missing registered external supplies block filing. |
| F06 — High | “Record GRA filing” could accept an unregistered or unreconciled month without fiscal-record attestation. | Require period registration/TIN, supported period, no blocking legacy/unclassified/unreconciled output or invalid input evidence, actual submission date and explicit reconciliation to approved fiscal records/authorised filing process. An acknowledgement remains a manual record, not electronic submission verification. |
| F07 — High | Guidance allowed prior-return errors to be netted into a later month, which is not a blanket statutory correction right. | Current-period adjustments require legal authority and cannot identify a different original period. An owner may open a GRA-authorised amendment against an original filing, with grounds, approval reference and an application within the section 63 three-month window. Reopen later filed months first and refile earlier months first, preserving all original snapshots. Cases outside that route require separate GRA/legal review. |
| F08 — High | Refunds had tax reversal data but no separately printable Fourth Schedule credit-note working paper; recipient TIN/address could not be captured. | Capture buyer tax identity in customer records and immutable invoice snapshot; printable invoice-linked credit note includes supplier/recipient identity, number, dates, reason, original/adjusted values and individual tax reversals. Missing recipient identity is explicitly shown. No fiscal signature/QR is fabricated. |
| F09 — Medium | Exclusive catalogue prices were shown to customers without an equally prominent tax-inclusive amount (section 68). General report headline net sales included tax while product/profit revenue excluded it. | Trusted catalogue responses provide tax-inclusive checkout display price; unclassified registered products cannot advertise a numeric untaxed price. Reports distinguish gross customer totals, recorded tax and tax-exclusive revenue. |
| F10 — High | Editable statutory rates/thresholds or disabling an existing obligation needed only a free-form reason. | Require legal/GRA authority for non-standard rates or thresholds, and effective cancellation authority for disabling a VAT obligation. Editing remains possible and audited; software does not validate the authenticity of a supplied legal document. |
| F11 — Medium | Duplicate supplier invoices could bypass checks using letter case/spaces. Multiple opening-credit entries could duplicate carry-forward. | Normalise supplier TIN/reference keys; reject a second opening-credit entry and require an evidenced correction. Statutory evidence authenticity and cross-business duplicate detection still require external verification. |
| F12 — High | Configurations with the same effective date/timestamp depended on local row order, which can differ after cloud recovery. The original workbook hid input evidence in a JSON field. | Monotonic configuration revision stored in synced payload; recovery test reverses config row order. Add structured input, certificate/payment and filing schedules, with explicit filing blockers in the export. |

## Remaining gates — do not hide these

1. **E-VAT integration is not built or certified.** The PDF receipt/credit note is a working paper until reconciled to the GRA-approved process. GRA fiscal keys, invoice acknowledgement/QR/signatures, approved offline recovery and UAT/sign-off are outstanding.
2. **No direct VAT return submission or payment API is implemented.** Workbook sheets are not a claimed approved GRA upload schema. Confirm current return fields and how VAT/levy credits are allocated or offset; separate traceable component schedules do not by themselves prove remittance-field approval.
3. **Amendment commencement:** obtain the enacted Act 1177 and applicable commencement or written authoritative GRA clarification before service-threshold automation. Existing obligations continue during review.
4. **Manual evidence:** supplier tax invoices, customs documents where applicable, withholding certificates, on-hand stock and legal authorities require verification by the taxpayer/adviser. Checkbox attestation cannot establish authenticity. Purchase cost recovery is not a retrospective inventory-revaluation system.
5. **Due dates:** displayed dates adjust weekends but remain expressly provisional for Ghana public holidays and GRA notices. There is no automatic tax remittance or interest/penalty assessment.
6. **Scope:** domestic retail sales and working papers, not a general ledger, customs/reverse-charge/digital-services compliance engine, withholding-agent remittance system, sector-levy engine or automated GRA assessment/refund application.
7. **Historical exposure:** a late discovery of VAT liability does not rewrite original transactions or silently invent collection. Filing blocks until the original-period discrepancies are reconciled through the authorised process.

8. **Historical correction boundaries:** automatic reconciliation of a missing inclusive-sale tax snapshot preserves the original and produces separate adjustments. Already-issued unreconciled historical credit notes and exclusive-price undercollection needing a corrected invoice/receivable must remain blocked for adviser/GRA review; the POS must not invent collection or a fiscal acknowledgement.
9. **Mansora’s own subscription billing:** the administration billing engine has a separate generic `taxBps` calculation. This POS audit does not establish Mansora’s own registration, separate VAT/levy invoicing or corporate return compliance; do not assume it inherits each shop’s VAT settings.
