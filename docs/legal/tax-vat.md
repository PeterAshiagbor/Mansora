# Tax and VAT notes

## The VAT Act, 2025 (Act 1151), in force from 1 January 2026

As understood at 2026-11-14 (confirm each point):
- Standard rate: VAT 15%, plus NHIL 2.5% and GETFund levy 2.5%, all charged on the same value: 20% in total.
- NHIL and GETFund are again deductible as input tax.
- The COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy is abolished.
- The VAT flat-rate scheme (the 3% / 4% scheme for retailers) is abolished, so retailers either register for standard
  VAT or are below the threshold.
- Registration threshold for suppliers of goods: GHS 750,000 turnover in 12 months. [Confirm whether suppliers of
  services must register whatever their turnover.]
- Some supplies are exempt (for example certain unprocessed foods, some medical supplies). The list is in the Act's schedule.

## What this means for shops (task C46)

- Most small shops are below the threshold and charge no VAT. The app must not show tax on their receipts.
- A registered shop needs: its TIN on receipts; each tax shown separately; prices that can be entered tax-inclusive;
  products marked standard-rated or exempt; a monthly report of output tax for the return.
- Rates must be settings with a start date, not fixed in code, so a future change does not need a new release.
- GRA's E-VAT system requires some taxpayers to issue invoices through certified software connected to GRA. Find out
  which shops are covered and how software is certified before promising it.

## What this means for Mansora

- Ask the adviser whether Mansora must register for VAT now (service provider), and from when.
- If registered: invoices must show Mansora's TIN, VAT, NHIL and GETFund separately, and the issuer settings need these fields.
- Fees in the terms should say whether they include taxes.
- Larger clients may have to withhold tax on Mansora's fees. Find out the rate and how to show it on receipts.
- Keep invoices and payment records for the period the Revenue Administration Act, 2016 (Act 915) requires (assumed six years).
